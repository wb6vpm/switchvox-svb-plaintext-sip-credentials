# Observed Production Incident

## Executive Summary

CVE-2026-45362 was discovered during response to an active toll-fraud incident affecting multiple subaccounts at the upstream carrier. Unauthorized calls authenticated with valid SIP carrier credentials. Responders rotated the affected credentials, but the replacement credentials were subsequently used for additional unauthorized calls.

During the same response window, Switchvox logs reviewed by the researcher showed that a fresh `.svb` backup had been created and downloaded after the credential rotation. Independent testing then established that backups generated by affected Switchvox releases stored SIP usernames, passwords, carrier addresses, and related trunk configuration in plaintext.

The timing and tested vulnerability mechanics strongly support the assessment that the downloaded backup was used to recover the replacement credentials. The original Switchvox log files have not yet been recovered for public preservation, so this report does not claim that the backup was conclusively the only credential-acquisition path or that the identity behind the backup request has been established.

This was not a theoretical-impact exercise. Carrier fraud controls suspended outbound calling for customers under the affected account structure for approximately 48 hours.

## Confirmed Facts

- Actual toll fraud occurred across multiple carrier subaccounts.
- The unauthorized calls authenticated with valid SIP carrier credentials.
- Responders rotated the credentials.
- The replacement credentials were subsequently abused.
- Switchvox logs reviewed during response showed a new backup creation and download after rotation.
- The upstream provider's response disrupted outbound service for legitimate customers for approximately 48 hours.
- Authorized testing independently demonstrated that affected `.svb` backups contained usable SIP provider credentials in plaintext.

## Analytical Assessment

The observed sequence is strongly consistent with the replacement credentials being recovered from the newly generated backup:

1. The original valid credentials were abused.
2. Those credentials were replaced.
3. A new backup was generated and downloaded after the rotation.
4. The replacement credentials were then abused.

Testing established that backups generated by affected Switchvox releases with a configured SIP provider contain the current provider username, password, carrier address, and trunk context needed for external SIP authentication. The vulnerability therefore provides a direct and repeatable mechanism for the observed sequence.

## Evidentiary Limits

The following points have not been established to forensic certainty:

- the identity of the person or system that initiated the backup creation and download
- the attacker's initial access method
- the downloaded backup as the exclusive possible source of the replacement credentials
- a complete timestamp-by-timestamp correlation from preserved raw Switchvox logs

These limitations narrow the attribution claim. They do not convert the confirmed toll fraud, valid-credential abuse, renewed abuse after rotation, or service disruption into hypothetical outcomes.

## Exploitation Status

As of 2026-09-03, the [CISA ADP enrichment attached to the CVE record](https://cveawg.mitre.org/api/cve/CVE-2026-45362) labels exploitation as `none`. The researcher disputes that assessment based on the production incident documented here.

The strongest supportable characterization is that actual toll fraud led directly to discovery of CVE-2026-45362, and the contemporaneously observed incident sequence strongly supports exploitation of plaintext credentials contained in a newly generated and downloaded Switchvox backup.

## Privacy and Publication

This report is intentionally sanitized. It does not publish:

- production SIP usernames or passwords
- customer names or complete account identifiers
- unredacted call-detail records
- unredacted provider correspondence
- customer backups

Sensitive supporting material should be shared only through an appropriate private channel with organizations evaluating the incident or coordinating vulnerability status.
