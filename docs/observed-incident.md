# Observed Production Incident

## Executive Summary

CVE-2026-45362 was discovered during analysis following a carrier-confirmed toll-fraud incident involving multiple independent Switchvox PBX appliances. Each affected PBX used a separate SIP subaccount at the upstream carrier. The carrier attributed unauthorized calls to the affected account and SIP subaccounts. Responders rotated the SIP credentials assigned to each affected PBX's carrier subaccount, but the carrier later continued to attribute unauthorized traffic to affected SIP subaccounts after those rotations.

During the same response window, the researcher reviewed audit entries on every affected Switchvox PBX and observed that a fresh `.svb` backup had been created and downloaded after the associated SIP credentials were rotated. Independent testing then established that backups generated by affected Switchvox releases stored SIP usernames, passwords, carrier addresses, and related trunk configuration in plaintext.

The repeated timing across the affected PBXs and the independently reproduced vulnerability mechanics strongly support the assessment that replacement credentials were recovered from the newly generated and downloaded backups. The post-rotation backup creation and download events on every affected PBX are based on the researcher's direct observations during incident response. The researcher did not separately export the audit entries for long-term preservation during the active response, when containment, credential rotation, and service restoration were the immediate priorities. The raw entries have not since been recovered, so the underlying artifacts are not currently available for independent reexamination. Accordingly, this report does not identify the person or system that initiated the backup activity or claim that the downloaded backups were the exclusive credential-acquisition path. That limitation applies to attribution, not to the observed events.

This was not a theoretical-impact exercise. Carrier fraud controls suspended outbound calling for customers under the affected account structure for approximately 48 hours.

## Confirmed Facts

- Actual toll fraud occurred across SIP subaccounts assigned to multiple independent Switchvox PBX appliances.
- Each affected SIP subaccount corresponded to a separate Switchvox PBX.
- The carrier attributed the unauthorized calls to the affected account and SIP subaccounts.
- Responders rotated the SIP credentials assigned to each affected PBX's carrier subaccount.
- The carrier later continued to attribute unauthorized traffic to affected SIP subaccounts after those rotations.
- On every affected PBX, Switchvox audit entries reviewed during response showed creation and download of a new backup after the associated credential rotation.
- The upstream provider's response disrupted outbound service for legitimate customers for approximately 48 hours.
- Authorized testing independently demonstrated that affected `.svb` backups contained usable SIP provider credentials in plaintext.
- Retained carrier fraud correspondence, the signed warning letter, the suspension and restoration messages, and an Industry Traceback Group request remain available for private review.

## Analytical Assessment

The observed sequence is strongly consistent with the replacement credentials being recovered from the newly generated backups:

1. The carrier attributed unauthorized traffic to SIP subaccounts assigned to multiple independent Switchvox PBX appliances.
2. Responders rotated the SIP credentials assigned to each affected PBX's carrier subaccount.
3. On every affected PBX, a new backup was generated and downloaded after the associated credential rotation.
4. The carrier later continued to attribute unauthorized traffic to affected SIP subaccounts after those rotations.

Testing established that backups generated by affected Switchvox releases with a configured SIP provider contain the current provider username, password, carrier address, and trunk context needed for external SIP authentication. The vulnerability therefore provides a direct and repeatable mechanism consistent with the observed multi-appliance sequence.

## Evidentiary Limits

The following points have not been established to forensic certainty:

- the identity of any person or system that initiated a backup creation or download
- the attacker's initial access method
- the downloaded backups as the exclusive possible source of the replacement credentials
- a complete timestamp-by-timestamp correlation from the raw Switchvox audit entries, which have not been recovered
- standalone raw carrier authentication logs for every reported call

These limitations narrow the attribution claim. They do not convert the confirmed toll fraud, post-rotation backup activity observed on every affected PBX, continued unauthorized traffic after the associated credential rotations, or service disruption into hypothetical outcomes. Based on the carrier and account records available during the response, the researcher's contemporaneous assessment was that the replacement credentials had been reacquired and used.

## Surviving Corroboration

The retained carrier record includes:

- an initial fraud case opened on December 3, 2025
- repeated carrier notices from December 4 through December 12 that the unauthorized traffic continued
- a December 17 notice suspending outbound termination because of traffic the carrier characterized as ongoing illegal impersonation
- a signed December 17 due-diligence warning concerning potentially fraudulent or noncompliant traffic
- a December 19 message removing the suspension after mitigation
- a retained Industry Traceback Group request characterizing a reported call as financial impersonation involving evidence of fraud, brand impersonation, and spoofing
- contemporaneous response messages documenting credential replacement for each affected PBX's carrier subaccount and conversion of affected subaccounts to source-IP authentication

These materials corroborate the fraud, carrier attribution, industry traceback activity, mitigation work, and operational disruption. They do not substitute for the unrecovered raw Switchvox audit entries or permit independent reexamination of those entries. They also do not independently establish exploitation of the backup flaw or prove the downloaded backups as the exclusive credential-acquisition path. That connection rests on the researcher's contemporaneous observation of post-rotation backup creation and download on every affected PBX, the incident-time assessment of unauthorized use after the associated credential rotations, and later authorized reproduction of plaintext credential recovery from affected backups.

## Exploitation Status

As of 2026-09-03, the [CISA ADP enrichment attached to the CVE record](https://cveawg.mitre.org/api/cve/CVE-2026-45362) labels exploitation as `none`. The researcher disputes that assessment based on the production incident documented here.

The strongest supportable characterization is that actual toll fraud led directly to discovery of CVE-2026-45362, and the contemporaneously observed multi-appliance incident sequence strongly supports exploitation of plaintext credentials contained in newly generated and downloaded Switchvox backups.

The requested correction and supporting rationale are documented in [CISA SSVC Exploitation Reassessment Request](cisa-ssvc-correction-request.md).
